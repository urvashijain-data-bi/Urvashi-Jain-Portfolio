My story

From the courtroom to the dashboard

From 2014 to 2021 I practiced law at the High Court of Judicature at Allahabad, the appellate court just below India’s Supreme Court. Much of my work was in banking: representing banks and borrowers in debt recovery, securities and negotiable-instrument disputes, and managing portfolios of cases from filing to resolution.

[In one or two sentences, in your own words: what made you move from law into business and data.] I earned an LLM at the University of Georgia, then an MBA in Business Analytics at the University of Delaware, where I was elected President of the MBA Student Association and received the MBA Leadership Award. At Barclays I worked on the kind of credit portfolios I used to argue about in court — this time across half a million accounts.

Today I build BI solutions, and the habits I formed as a lawyer still shape how I work.

Evidence before conclusions

A case only holds if the record supports it. I trace every KPI back to its source logic before anyone relies on it.

Making complex things clear

Arguing before judges taught me to lead with the point. I bring the same clarity to executive reporting and user training.

Finding common ground

As a certified mediator I learned to align people with different goals, which is the heart of good requirements gathering.
